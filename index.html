Spiritual Progression Portfolio

Three Guiding Principles

Keeping Jesus Christ at the Center

Jesus Christ must remain at the center of every decision, ambition, and responsibility. In cybersecurity, this principle is most direct: every access control decision, every incident response, and every vulnerability disclosure involves questions about who is affected, who bears the risk, and who is responsible. Focusing on what has lasting value rather than temporary advantage means prioritizing genuine protection over the appearance of compliance, and honoring the people whose data I am entrusted to secure over the convenience of cutting corners.

"Lay not up for yourselves treasures upon earth... but lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven." — 3 Nephi 13:19–20

Lifelong Learning, Personal Growth, and Diligence

Cloud technology changes faster than almost any other domain in IT. The professional who stops learning stops being relevant within years. President Uchtdorf's example of Daniel in Babylon captures the posture this principle requires: maintaining faith and commitment in an environment that does not always reward it, knowing that faithfulness in preparation is what readies us for the moments when our skills and character are genuinely needed. Come what may, and love it — including the unfamiliar, the difficult, and the humbling.

"Men should be anxiously engaged in a good cause, and do many things of their own free will." — D&C 58:27

Serving Others through Integrity, Resilience, and Christlike Love

Systems administrators are stewards of the infrastructure that organizations and their people depend on. Behind every technical issue is a person depending on those systems. Elder Wirthlin's four principles for facing adversity, laughing, seeking the eternal perspective, trusting in compensation, and putting trust in God, describe the resilience I want to bring into every professional environment. How I react to adversity, to system failures, to security incidents, to demanding clients, is a major factor in how effective and trustworthy I will be as a professional.

"When ye are in the service of your fellow beings ye are only in the service of your God." — Mosiah 2:17

Reflective Activity

Personal Reflections on Agency and Growth

I Have Been Building Longer Than I Realized

During my reflective activity this week, the thought that stood out most clearly was this: I have been building longer than I realized. When I looked honestly at the journey from electrical maintenance at Volta Aluminium to IT systems administration and cybersecurity, I did not see a career change. I saw a single continuous thread of learning to maintain systems that people depend on. The motors and switchgear I have maintained for over a decade and the cloud infrastructure, intrusion detection systems, and Linux servers I am now learning to build are different technologies, but they require the same foundational qualities: precision, accountability, patience under pressure, and the humility to keep learning.

Elder Wirthlin's counsel to come what may and love it describes the posture I had to develop to keep moving forward during the most demanding seasons of this degree. Elder Uchtdorf's challenge to be not afraid, only believe describes the faith required to pursue a career in IT in Ghana, where the path is not always validated by the environment around me. And the Problem Solving and Collaboration reading's emphasis on the outward mindset describes the kind of leader I want to become: one who stays focused on the people around me rather than my own position or progress.

Spiritually, what developed most during this reflection was my understanding that my faith has not been separate from my professional journey. It has been woven through it. The discipline of prayer that helped me stay grounded during difficult coursework, the covenant-keeping that kept me honest when shortcuts were available, and the service orientation that shaped how I treated clients during my internship were not additions to my professional development. They were part of its structure. The integration of my spiritual and professional identities is not a project that ends with this course. It is the ongoing work of a disciple-leader.
